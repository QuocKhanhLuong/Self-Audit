# Falsifiable experiment plan for correction-state architectures

Research-only proposal, 2026-09-11, anchored to verified `main` `35aaba9335e0d8a0aa344356e20e93ae902b950d`. No experiments below have been run. Thresholds, margins, seeds, budgets, and pass/fail criteria are **proposed preregistration choices**, not empirical findings. Implementation, training, data-access verification, and GPU profiling remain future work.

The primary question is whether accepted correction history helps the next correction **at the same retained soft annotation**, while reducing destruction of earlier fixes. A separate question is whether a compact complete system improves the accuracy/resource frontier relative to the retained pretrained ConvNeXt baseline. These questions require separate experiments; a weaker A0 can create more correction headroom without producing a better final model.

## 3. A0, capacity, and budget matching

**Mechanism experiment:** one frozen H/A0 producer supplies exactly the same complete soft logits and visual features to every correction arm. Cache provenance includes checkpoint hash, model/config/source hash, patient/phase/slice ids, preprocessing, dtype, and feature/annotation checksum. Different wrappers cannot change logits, entropy, turn index, latest audit evidence, padding, or crop geometry. Train compatible correction modules on this common interface. For cross-architecture prefix interventions, teacher-force the same recorded accepted annotation/audit sequence while each architecture constructs its own latent history; then hold the retained annotation identical and branch only the future. Record that this is a controlled-prefix estimand and report ordinary on-policy results separately. A0 is identical by construction; the frozen shared encoder still counts in total parameters and once per inference.

**Whole-system experiment:** jointly train each actual encoder/core combination, reporting patient A0 and final performance, net correction gain, and the distribution of initial errors. A proposed A0 adequacy margin is 0.5 Dice percentage points below the retained pretrained comparator; a tighter same-budget within-stem comparison targets 0.2 points. These are research utility margins, not clinical equivalence margins. Declare A0 matched only when the paired confidence interval lies within the registered equivalence band. Overlapping ordinary confidence intervals or selecting similarly scored patients is insufficient. If A0 differs, stratify by **common reference** A0 difficulty and report both raw final quality and gain; do not attribute the gain difference solely to memory.

Parameter and MAC matching are separate axes. For a parameter-matched comparison target total counts within 5% and disclose actual mismatch; for compute-matched comparisons target total inference MACs within 10%, with identical input resolution and maximum attempts. If both cannot be satisfied simultaneously, report separate parameter-matched and compute-matched experiments/Pareto points. Match accepted turns only for a diagnostic forced-prefix experiment; naturally different acceptance/halting rates are policy outcomes and must be reported.

The shared analytical reference is roughly 4.147/9.315/14.275M raw A weights for the 5/10/15M regimes, before omitted small modules; ordinary A turns including decoder/auditor are roughly 1.075/2.404/3.266 GMAC. B/C can exceed the regime ceilings after writers, heads, and overhead. Use the explicit reserves and width-reduction examples in [training_strategy.md](training_strategy.md); no raw figure is a measured complete-system count. Keep the native ConvNeXt control even when it is outside these compact tiers. A large encoder plus a 5M correction head is not a 5M system.

Every comparison fixes a maximum of three attempted refinement turns, image size 256x256, class order, augmentation, selected-labelled patients, trainable/frozen modules, inference precision, and no test-time augmentation in the primary run. Sweep caps 0/1/2/3 as a diagnostic; a cap of 5 may be a separately preregistered stability test. CC/pathway/restitution internal forward passes count against a separate total proposal/audit compute budget, even if the external API calls them one turn.

## 4. Exact outcome definitions

### 4.1 Patient segmentation and transition sign

For patient `i`, phase `p`, class `c in {RV,MYO,LV}`, define hard volume Dice

```text
D_i,p,c(A) = 2*|pred_c(A) intersect Y_c| / (|pred_c(A)| + |Y_c|).
```

Both masks empty gives 1; exactly one empty gives 0. This is a stated convention, not smoothing by an arbitrary epsilon. Primary `Q_i(A)` averages the three classes within each available predeclared ED/ES phase, then averages phases (normally six values). Cohort `Q` is the unweighted patient mean. Report class/phase components and empty-mask counts. Final gain is `G_i=Q_i(A_final)-Q_i(A0)`, in Dice units; multiply by 100 only for percentage-point presentation.

For an attempted **slice** transition `r=(i,p,z,t)`, use the same fixed three-class Dice formula on the slice, with the stated empty convention, to define `g_r=Q_slice(C_r)-Q_slice(A_r)`. Default proposed neutral tolerance `epsilon=0.001` (0.1 Dice points) is frozen before experiments: beneficial `b_r=1[g_r>epsilon]`, harmful `h_r=1[g_r<-epsilon]`, neutral otherwise. Report a sensitivity at epsilon=0 as secondary. Pixel FIX/REGRESS rates below do not depend on this tolerance.

The retained baseline's auditor may use its documented multiclass training target, including background. Keep that target for exact baseline reproduction; archive it and calibrate/evaluate its predicted score against the declared foreground endpoint. A new foreground-target auditor is a separate labelled target-contract ablation. Never compare scores from differently defined DeltaQ targets as if identically calibrated. Slice improvements do not add linearly to volume Dice; patient final quality is computed from reconstructed volumes, not summed slice `g_r`.

Let `R` contain only active attempted rows, including the first rejected proposal and excluding padding or any turns after HALT. Let `a_r=1` if accepted. For each patient aggregate the following numerator/denominator pairs across its phases, slices, and attempts:

| Metric | Exact numerator / denominator | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Beneficial proposal rate | `sum b_r / |R|` | Quality of generated proposals |
| Beneficial acceptance precision | `sum a_r*b_r / sum a_r` | Beneficial fraction among commits |
| Beneficial acceptance recall | `sum a_r*b_r / sum b_r` | Beneficial attempted proposals retained |
| Beneficial rejection rate | `sum (1-a_r)*b_r / sum b_r` | Useful proposals discarded, among observed beneficial proposals |
| Harmful acceptance fraction | `sum a_r*h_r / sum a_r` | Harm among commits |
| Harmful escape rate | `sum a_r*h_r / sum h_r` | Fraction of harmful attempted proposals accepted |
| Acceptance coverage | `sum a_r / |R|` | Work actually committed |
| Accepted signed slice gain | `sum a_r*g_r / sum a_r` | Mean quality change per commit, not patient final gain |
| Harmful patient incidence | `#patients with >=1 harmful acceptance / #evaluated patients` | Cluster-level harm summary, including patients that accept nothing |

Report both patient-macro rates (mean over patients with nonzero denominator, with eligible-patient count) and pooled numerator/denominator ratios (pixel/transition-weighted descriptive summaries). The primary inferential estimand is patient-macro; pooled summaries must not masquerade as patient weighting. Zero denominator is `NA`, never zero, perfect precision, or a pseudocount. A run that accepts nothing has zero coverage, NA accepted-harm fraction, and cannot pass a beneficial-correction gate. Rejection prevents observing later proposals; do not call beneficial rejection recall a recall over unvisited possible futures.

### 4.2 Pixel repair, regression, and previous-fix destruction

For valid non-ignored pixels `v in Omega_r`, define `ok_before(v)=1[argmax A_r(v)=Y(v)]` and `ok_after(v)=1[argmax C_r(v)=Y(v)]`:

```text
F_r = {v: not ok_before(v) and ok_after(v)}
E_r = {v: ok_before(v) and not ok_after(v)}
W_r = {v: not ok_before(v)}; O_r = {v: ok_before(v)}
```

Wrong-to-different-wrong pixels are in neither F nor E. Beneficial pixel correction rate is `sum |F_r| / sum |W_r|`; regression rate is `sum |E_r| / sum |O_r|`. Restrict numerator **and denominator** by `a_r` for accepted-only versions. Report `(|F_r|-|E_r|)/|Omega_r|` as net correctness change, not as DeltaDice. Report all pixels plus foreground-adjacent/class-specific strata so background abundance cannot conceal boundary harm. For class-specific FIX use `Y=c`; for REGRESS the previously correct class also equals `Y=c`. Define any spatial boundary band in millimetres when spacing is known, otherwise pixels, with the width registered before scoring.

Maintain a separate GT analysis ledger for each slice; it is never model input. Before turn t, let `EverFixed_t` contain pixels fixed by any earlier accepted transition, and `LiveFix_t=EverFixed_t intersect O_t`. Initially both are empty. For the current proposal:

```text
Destroyed_t = LiveFix_t intersect E_t
EverFixed_(t+1) = EverFixed_t union F_t          if accepted
                 EverFixed_t                   if rejected
```

Current correctness is recomputed from the retained annotation. A previously destroyed pixel can later become live again if repaired; this definition intentionally allows repeated destruction events. **Previous-fix destruction rate** is `sum a_t*|Destroyed_t| / sum a_t*|LiveFix_t|`; the attempted-proposal variant omits `a_t` from both terms. Preservation is `1-rate` only when the denominator is positive. A final quality gain can coexist with nonzero destruction, so report both.

Also report first-ever destruction: count unique pixels destroyed by an accepted later transition for the first time, divided by unique ever-fixed pixels that had at least one subsequent **attempted** correction opportunity. A fix on the final turn with no later attempt is right-censored from this denominator. At-risk opportunity is defined before acceptance, so rejecting the next proposal counts as successful protection for this policy-level metric. Keep attempted-proposal destruction and accepted-event destruction separate. Final retention `|EverFixed_final intersect correct_final|/|EverFixed_final|` is descriptive; it includes late fixes with little exposure and does not replace opportunity-normalized destruction.

Store raw integer counts per patient, turn and class. Undefined live-fix denominators, the number of patients with prior accepted fixes, and the distribution of opportunities must accompany every destruction result. Training masks in B/C use the same `LiveFix` definition with detached GT; inference memory uses predicted semantics and may disagree with this analysis ledger.

### 4.3 Auditor quality, chain length, and successful turns

Compute on-policy Auditor AUROC/AUPRC on **all active attempted transitions** in R, including the first rejected transition; accepted-only scores are a separate selection-conditioned diagnostic. For beneficial discrimination, target `b_r` and score `q_r`; neutrals and harmful cases are negative. For harmful discrimination, target `h_r` and score `-q_r`. Report prevalence, positive/negative counts, threshold, patient support and score-target contract. AUROC is NA if either class is absent; AUPRC is NA without positives, and with only positives is a degenerate value of 1 flagged as nondiscriminative. Use average precision as the declared PR summary, rather than silently mixing trapezoidal PR integration. Patient-bootstrap intervals preserve all transitions; give per-patient macro and pooled descriptive scores separately. Synthetic/off-policy stress sets never replace or silently mix into the on-policy result.

Report Pearson correlation of `q_r` with `g_r` and Spearman correlation using average ranks for ties. Either is NA for fewer than three transitions or a constant score/target vector (including constant ranks); do not return zero as a default. Use patient-specific correlations with eligible-patient counts and a separately labelled pooled correlation; a mean patient correlation is not the correlation of patient means. The same rules apply to the accepted-only subset. Local FIX/REGRESS confusion metrics in Section 5 use all active attempted pixels before any accepted-only restriction.

For each full inference row j (every valid centre slice, including zero-acceptance and cap-zero rows), accepted-chain length is `k_j=sum_t a_j,t`, attempted length is the number of active attempted turns, and successful-correction turns are `s_j=sum_t a_j,t*b_j,t`. Immediate HALT makes the accepted chain contiguous. Report `sum k_j/N_rows`, `sum attempted_j/N_rows`, and `sum s_j/N_rows`, plus patient-macro versions averaging rows within each patient first; denominators include all evaluated rows/patients, not only active-at-turn-t or accepting rows. Also report `Pr(k_j=k)`, `Pr(s_j>=1)`, and reach probability for each turn with N_rows as the denominator. Gate precision/recall retain their different denominators from Section 4.1. No additional successful turn is imputed after HALT.

### 4.4 Correction headroom and selection headroom

At an identical frozen prefix `(H,A,S,t)`, build a registered finite candidate pool `C_1...C_K` with exactly the same generation budget for all selectors. Include no-op `C_0=A`, so `g_0=0`. For each prefix:

```text
oracle_pool_gain = max_{k=0..K} g_k
selected_gain = g_(selector's chosen k), using k=0 on rejection
selection_regret = oracle_pool_gain - selected_gain
```

Correction headroom is the patient-averaged `oracle_pool_gain` on the common-prefix bank; report beneficial-candidate availability and candidate `K`. Selection headroom is the remaining gap between that oracle and a specified predicted selector on the **same** bank. Oracle realization fraction `mean(selected_gain)/mean(oracle_pool_gain)` is NA if the denominator is zero; it may be negative, and is not clipped. This is an upper bound within the finite offered pool, not a universal bound on trainable correction capacity.

Add a prefix-oracle diagnostic on a frozen always-accept trajectory: the best GT-scored prefix including A0. It can identify whether a useful intermediate proposal existed, but later candidates depend on earlier commits. A closed-loop GT gate must recompute proposals after each oracle decision and is a separate nondeployable analysis. Never substitute static prefix selection for a causal closed-loop oracle or take the larger of several post-hoc oracle definitions without disclosure. Synthetic GT-edited candidates establish an auditor stress test, not learned correction headroom.

Also distinguish **score-ceiling headroom** `H_i=1-Q_i(A0)` from finite-pool oracle headroom. Ceiling capture is `(Q_i(A_final)-Q_i(A0))/H_i`, NA when `H_i=0`; report unweighted patient-macro capture over eligible patients and pooled `sum G_i/sum H_i` separately. Negative capture is retained. The ideal Dice ceiling ignores achievable model capacity, annotation noise and the available action pool, so a low ceiling capture does not prove the proposed updater can realize the missing gain. Finite-pool realization from the previous equation answers that narrower offered-candidate question.

## 5. State-usefulness interventions and falsifiers

At each eligible accepted prefix with at least one possible future turn, clone `H`, the **full retained soft logits** `A_t`, most recent predicted audit map/score, turn index, active mask, visual skips, and RNG. Clone parameters and freeze them. Check equality of these tensors and non-state side effects before running any arm. Merely matching argmax masks, patient Dice, or A0 scores does not hold annotation information constant.

| Intervention | What changes | What a positive result could establish |
|---|---|---|
| True state vs zero/no-history reset | Only latent/history state and its validity representation | Extra state matters beyond current annotation/evidence; training includes omission exposure to reduce out-of-distribution reset effects |
| True accepted write vs null write | Both arms retain identical accepted annotation; null arm keeps the earlier state | The most recent write is useful, not merely having a persistent tensor |
| Latest-only vs complete bounded history | Same annotation and current audit; older entries removed | Older accepted history contributes beyond the last audit |
| Correction tensor reset with semantic ledgers retained | Only unstructured tensor changes | Value of continuous correction features beyond explicit class semantics |
| Semantic ledgers reset with correction tensor retained | Only class-bound repair/preservation maps change | Value of semantic memory beyond generic recurrence |
| Class binding removed; exact B1 + one ConvGRU | Compare unbound maps or the same ledgers with one recurrent branch and identical losses | Whether class obligations and the split latent structure each contribute |
| FIX/REGRESS swap; spatial permutation; temporal-order permutation | Keep total map mass/count/age distribution where possible, change one semantic axis at a time | Dependence on correct semantics/location/order rather than channel magnitude or extra capacity |
| Same-patient shuffled accepted history | Same number, dtype and tensor norms, wrong turn association | Temporal specificity; avoids patient identity as a trivial cue |
| Matched-difficulty cross-patient history swap | H/A/turn unchanged; history donor from fit-only predeclared bank for evaluation | Stress test for anatomy specificity; an out-of-distribution negative control, not the sole causal evidence |
| Parameter-matched generic ConvGRU / generic-gated memory | Same width/compute and audit access, no semantic split | Whether B's structure contributes beyond recurrence and conditioning |

Generate one next proposal in all arms and score it before gate selection; this isolates proposer utility from changes in acceptance. Then run a fixed **remaining** hard-policy horizon in each arm with its own predicted decisions, retaining the common starting A. Define one-step utility `U1_i=Q_i(C_true)-Q_i(C_intervention)` after reconstructing the compared slice results; define policy utility `UK_i=Q_i(A_final,true)-Q_i(A_final,intervention)`. When intervening on one slice prefix, hold every untouched slice of the reference volume identical in both reconstructions. For multiple eligible prefixes, average within patient first; use a reference bank of prefixes generated independently of the compared arm for the primary contrast. A prefix bank generated only by B can favour B's state distribution; repeat on A/common-bank prefixes or explicitly limit the estimand to B-reachable histories.

Count equal downstream proposals/audits for fixed-horizon diagnostic arms and report actual naturally halted compute separately. For C's memory deletion, keep factual A independently supplied; changing the annotation through event removal/replacement is a **restitution** experiment. The primary C state pair first computes and accepts the same counterfactual-minus-factual logit innovation from the most recent ordinary event, then gives both arms those identical new logits: one commits the matching latent innovation and the other leaves its latent accumulator unchanged. Recompute subsequent proposals under the same remaining horizon. This tests latent transfer beyond the existing output-space CC effect. Pair actual restitution against no-op, direct rollback, coordinate-only CC and equal-compute random support search. Do not mix annotation changes into the memory utility estimate.

C's required attribution checks follow [candidate_C.md](candidate_C.md): rerun the same ordinary action from its recorded pre-state with factual support before replacing its latent/logit contribution using changed support. Only the most recent ordinary accepted event is eligible; no older-event deletion after intervening actions, no recursive restitution, and no descendant replay. Class ledgers follow the actual current-to-restituted audit, not a reconstructed historical audit. At beta=1 test immediate-event additive identity; beta<1 is interpolation. **Mandatory counterexample control:** ordinary deterministic snapshot replacement `G(S_before,s_counterfactual)` in A/B, with the same saved pre-state, output constraints and compute budget. For the immediate last event this can equal the additive replacement, defeating an additive-architecture novelty claim. C must show extra future value beyond that comparator and positive latent-transfer utility at identical logits. Reject stale records, mismatch in normalization/batch/weights/dtype/turn, failed output preservation constraints, and unbounded support changes; record the declared ordinary fallback when applicable.

Report restitution eligibility `eligible attempts / all active refinement attempts`, factual replay failure `failed factual replays / initiated factual replays`, fallback frequency `ordinary fallbacks / initiated restitution attempts`, identity frequency `zero-innovation feasible proposals / feasible checked proposals`, and restitution acceptance `accepted restitution proposals / officially audited restitution proposals`. Every zero denominator is NA. Include per-patient counts and failure reasons, not only the successful replay subset. The one-coordinate-gradient/two-feasibility-check bound is part of the matched comparison: inner feasibility checks use recorded FIX/output/support constraints and no official Auditor calls; exactly one final official current-transition audit evaluates the selected restitution or ordinary fallback. Extra candidate audits are a separately costed protocol, not hidden in the two checks.

**Reject/HALT latent equivalence control:** compare retaining versus discarding the latent draft on the first rejected step, with identical previous annotation and immediate HALT. Returned annotations must match under deterministic inference and isolated buffers, because neither rejected latent state is read again. A difference is evidence of side effects, state leakage, or protocol changes; a tie is expected and gives no evidence for beneficial transaction rollback. If a counterfactual diagnostic continues after rejection, label it a different policy.

For semantic permutations, report predicted local-map calibration/confusion first. For each local class k, `precision_k=TP_k/(TP_k+FP_k)`, `recall_k=TP_k/(TP_k+FN_k)`, and `F1_k=2TP_k/(2TP_k+FP_k+FN_k)`; zero denominator is NA. Report FIX and REGRESS separately, confusion counts and patient support. A badly calibrated auditor can make correct semantics unusable, whereas an architecture that ignores all permutations has failed to show semantic dependence. Also train an identical-capacity model with consistently permuted labels/maps as a control for arbitrary channel relabelling: a fixed relabelling that the network learns is not destruction of information. The primary perturbation randomizes semantic association at evaluation within a known correctly trained model, complemented by trained nonsemantic controls.

### State drift, semantic conditioning, and persistence

For each latent correction tensor C with D entries, record proposed-write drift `rms_prop=||C_write_proposed-C_t||_2/sqrt(D)` and retained drift `rms_kept=||C_retained_next-C_t||_2/sqrt(D)`. Relative drift is `||Delta C||_2/||C_t||_2`, NA when the previous norm is zero; absolute RMS is still defined. Cosine similarity is `<C_t,C_next>/(||C_t||_2*||C_next||_2)`, NA when either tensor is zero. Compute each B branch separately plus their concatenation; include ledger-delta norms. Record the tentative decoder scratch state separately from the post-audit proposed persistent write, because B's no-event rule can suppress the latter.

Compare proposed drift for accepted versus rejected attempts; rejection's retained drift should be exactly zero and does not demonstrate semantic usefulness. Stratify by true FIX-heavy (`|F|>|E|`), REGRESS-heavy (`|E|>|F|`), and tied transitions, alongside separately labelled predicted-heavy strata based on summed predicted local probabilities. Use every active attempted row and give stratum/patient denominators; empty strata are NA. Correlate proposed drift with observed gain/destruction using the correlation rules above. Large drift, a cosine change or a separation between accepted/rejected drift is descriptive, not proof that memory helps.

Repeat the **same prefixes and annotations** with zero evidence, FIX/REGRESS swaps, spatial permutations, removed class binding and no-event inputs. Test their interaction with branch-specific proposed/retained drift and future utility. Unqualified/tied local audit outputs create no B1 event; fixed baseline argmax handles tied class logits. B's metadata identifies the last touch per class/map/location; its compressed ledgers do not identify every historical event.

Measure persistence of a useful accepted write on nonterminal prefixes by branching true-write versus null-write at the same post-acceptance annotation, then teacher-forcing the same subsequent accepted annotation/evidence sequence. At future lag l report `||C_true(k+l)-C_null(k+l)||_2 / ||C_true(k)-C_null(k)||_2` and cosine with the initial state difference, each NA for zero denominators. Stratify the originating write by GT-measured beneficial/harmful/neutral class and give the number of surviving lag opportunities. This controlled impulse response tests how long state differences persist without annotation divergence; an on-policy continuation is a separate outcome. Persistent differences may preserve mistakes, so pair this with prior-fix destruction and future-utility metrics, not a preference for maximum persistence.

For visual features V, compute the same RMS/relative/cosine diagnostics at each turn of a rollout. Cached immutable V should have exactly zero intra-rollout RMS drift by construction; nonzero drift indicates a contract failure or an explicitly different feature-refinement arm. Separately compare a fixed image/preprocessing batch across saved encoder training revisions in deterministic evaluation mode. Report per-scale RMS/cosine and A0 changes, using identical feature dimensions; do not compare arbitrary channels across different encoder architectures as if aligned. Changing encoder weights across training can change V despite correct within-rollout immutability.

### Future linear probes

Freeze a trained model and collect fit-patient prefix features. Fit linear/logistic probes using (i) common current information only `[H,P_t,entropy,e_t,t]`, (ii) state only, and (iii) their concatenation. Use pointwise 1x1 probes for spatial outcomes and identical pooled features for global outcomes. Probe targets are next-step beneficial/harmful labels, remaining error, and whether a live prior fix is destroyed within the remaining fixed horizon. Targets come from a frozen reference continuation independent of which probe is fit, and all classes/support denominators are reported.

Tune regularization on model-selection patients; lock probes before evaluation, use no evaluator-patient pixel subsampling in fitting, and compute AUROC/AUPRC/Brier at the patient-clustered level. Include equal-dimensional random projections/noise and shuffled targets as controls. If either binary class is absent, AUROC is NA; if no positive exists, AUPRC is NA. Predicting the current mask is a diagnostic of redundant storage, not future utility. Better incremental probes establish decodable predictive information, not causal use; a state claim requires the intervention outcomes too.

## 6. Held-out calibration and uncertainty

Freeze the model/checkpoint, target definition, `T_max`, policy, score direction and candidate generation before opening calibration data. Use a prespecified small signed-score threshold grid, e.g. `{-0.01,0,0.0025,0.005,0.01,0.02}`, and full sequential trajectories for each threshold. Select the threshold with greatest patient mean final-minus-A0 gain among those meeting proposed **empirical** calibration constraints: harmful acceptance fraction <=10%, at least five calibration patients accepting something, and >=20% attempted-transition acceptance coverage. Break ties toward higher threshold, then lower compute. This is an empirical operating-point proposal, not a certified clinical risk bound.

If no threshold is feasible, declare the calibrated gate unavailable, retain initial-only as the conservative output and report the failed coverage/risk condition. Do not tune a larger grid after examining evaluation data. Do not call a zero-acceptance solution a high-precision gate. Report accepted counts, transition/patient rates, all grid points and the calibration-selection optimism limitation. Score-probability calibration (e.g. logistic mapping) is optional and must be preregistered separately with held-out fit/assessment inside the calibration partition; ten patients may not support it. A signed DeltaQ is not inherently a beneficial probability.

Confidence intervals use **paired patient bootstrap**, proposed 10,000 replicates and a recorded resampling seed. For ACDC, resample patients within the five pathology strata, retaining all slices, phases, turns, interventions and arm predictions for each selected patient. For external M&Ms, resample within prespecified vendor strata, with centres described and patient clusters respected. Recompute ratio numerators/denominators inside every replicate; do not bootstrap per-slice ratios as independent cases. A resample with zero denominator is NA; report the proportion of such replicates and suppress an interval if fewer than 95% are defined.

For the primary complete-system comparison, average each patient's paired outcome difference over the same three registered training seeds, then bootstrap patients; this interval is conditional on those seeds. Report all seed-level effects and a secondary two-axis resampling of matched seeds and patients, with the instability from only three seeds explicit. Do not pool seeds as extra patients. A seed is not an independent medical subject, and a patient bootstrap does not establish robustness to all training randomness.

Use two-sided 95% percentile intervals for gains/differences and one-sided 95% upper bounds for declared harm limits. For zero rare events, a bootstrap interval can collapse spuriously to zero: report an exact binomial bound for **patient-level any-harm incidence** alongside the cluster bootstrap, explicitly a different estimand from transition harm. With zero harmed patients out of n, the one-sided upper bound is `1-0.05^(1/n)`; at n=20 it is about 13.9%. Thus small cohorts cannot establish very low absolute harm rates. Do not present transition count as the sample size for that bound.

Two confirmatory mechanism contrasts are proposed: B minus **M9 (exact B1 writer + one parameter-matched ConvGRU + identical losses)** in same-annotation policy utility, and B minus M9 in previous-fix destruction. A remains the broader unstructured baseline, but beating A alone cannot identify the contribution of B's split latent state. Apply Holm adjustment to these two planned tests if reporting significance; provide effect sizes/intervals regardless. Primary mechanism destruction uses the common-prefix live-fix risk set so denominators are shared before the next proposal. Policy-level accepted-only ratios can have different eligible patients; report their common eligible-patient intersection for paired contrasts, excluded-support counts and coverage shifts, without presenting that selected subset as an unconditional effect. Other semantic permutations, classes, label fractions, tiers, and probes are secondary/descriptive unless separately powered and registered. Model-selection and calibration reuse must be excluded from confirmatory test results. No sample-size adequacy is claimed without pilot variance and the registered meaningful effect.

## 7. Staged minimal experiment sequence

| Stage | Smallest useful experiment | Outputs / decision |
|---|---|---|
| E0: protocol feasibility | Read source/data manifests; shape/provenance/deterministic replay checks on bounded fixtures and a few fit patients when authorized | Establish software identities, actual params and operation accounting; no effectiveness claim, no GPU claim from CPU/synthetic checks |
| E1: A0 and correction headroom | M0 and M2 at 20 labelled fit patients, one pilot seed; same frozen A0 for M3/M4/M5 correction pilots; caps 0/1/3 | Inspect selection-only learning curves, A0 deficit and common-pool oracle headroom; choose one compact tier before the test set is opened |
| E2: early-audit curriculum | M0 versus M1, then M3 with late versus early auditing; default no-soft schedule; optional soft branch tested once | Separate architecture from schedule; training curves, exposure/compute ledger, real versus synthetic auditor quality, hard-policy readiness |
| E3: memory mechanism | M3/A, B with segmentation loss only, B with paired utility + destruction losses, M4/M9, and same-annotation null/reset/latest-write/quality-only controls | One-step/policy utility and destruction under equal annotations; semantic permutations and future probes; advance only if B beats the exact B1 + single-GRU control |
| E4: restitution and direct competitors | M6/M7/M8 core x CC cross; C replay tests, C without/with restitution, naive rollback, A/B deterministic snapshot replacement and same-compute repair; required literature adapters | Distinguish event memory, CC transfer, and added search/repair compute; require same-logit latent-transfer utility; no new-encoder explanation if benefits occur equally with ConvNeXt |
| E5: confirmatory compact systems | The selected simple baseline, B/C only if eligible, M0/M1/M2 and remaining mandatory comparators; three paired seeds at primary labelled cohort | Freeze all choices, calibrate once, evaluate the locked ACDC cohort once; confirm effects with patient intervals |
| E6: data/size/external robustness | Selected architectures at 10/20/40/60 labels and feasible 5/10/15M tiers; fixed pretrained control; locked ACDC-to-M&Ms inference | Predeclare the factorial subset before test access; report skipped cells as NOT RUN; no external retuning |

This is staged screening, not permission to drop losing mandatory baselines from a final superiority claim. E1-E4 pilot decisions use fit/model-selection patients only. E5/E6 hypotheses and exact surviving matrix are frozen together before evaluation; if resource limits prevent E6, the conclusion remains limited to E5's chosen cohort/tier. Native ConvNeXt is retained throughout. E3/E4 do not require training every encoder/tier combination: use a common cached H/A0 first. The minimum first serious result is M0 vs M1 plus A/M4/B on identical starts, with correct destruction denominators; it is not a full-system or external generalization result.

## 8. Proposed go/no-go gates

Margins here are research priorities, not clinical acceptability thresholds. Lock them before running and permit `INCONCLUSIVE` when intervals are too wide.

| Gate | Proposed go condition | No-go / inconclusive interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| G0: software and resource feasibility | Exact accepted-state semantics, reject/HALT equivalence, valid class mapping/splits, finite replay, and measured complete parameters under declared tier | Any violation blocks effectiveness interpretation; estimates alone cannot pass |
| G1: useful proposal headroom | Common-pool oracle mean gain >=0.002 Dice and paired patient CI lower bound >0 on model-selection diagnostics | If upper bound <=0.002, stop added memory complexity for that frozen producer; if wide, collect more evidence or report inconclusive |
| G2: early curriculum | M1 hard-policy final patient gain over M0 >=0.002 with lower CI >0, or equivalent quality within 0.002 and >=20% lower measured training compute | No advantage over late audit means early feedback is unsupported; count extra supervision/compute and optional soft-commit mismatch |
| G3: B memory usefulness | Same-annotation B utility over M9 (exact B1 + one matched GRU + identical losses) >=0.002, lower CI >0; destruction difference upper CI <0; latest-write/quality-only controls cannot explain the gain | Beating plain A alone is insufficient; no effect versus M9 defeats the split semantic-state claim; fewer eligible live fixes yields inconclusive, never zero destruction evidence |
| G4: C restitution | Exact replay checks pass; same-prefix final gain >=0.002 over A/B deterministic snapshot replacement under the same output constraints/cost, naive rollback and equal-compute repair, with lower CI >0 and no destruction increase; same-logit latent-transfer utility separately >=0.002 with lower CI >0 | Algebraic equivalence or failure of same-logit utility defeats additive-state novelty; report replay engineering only, and do not add excluded descendant replay to rescue it |
| G5: whole-system compact value | Final ACDC gain over same-budget baseline >=0.002 with lower CI >0 and A0 within the registered band; or final noninferiority within 0.002 with >=20% lower measured end-to-end latency | Better correction from worse A0 alone fails; compare native pretrained ConvNeXt on the actual Pareto frontier |
| G6: external retention | Frozen M&Ms paired final-quality difference lower CI >-0.005, report all vendor/phase effects, no external adaptation | Poor/uncertain transfer prevents a domain-generalization claim; cannot repair by recalibrating on M&Ms |

For G2-G6 additionally require no evidence of increased harmful acceptance versus the matched comparator: proposed one-sided upper CI for the patient-macro harmful-acceptance difference <=0.02, with coverage >=20% and eligible-support reporting. A lack of eligible commits or unstable interval is inconclusive. Report absolute patient-level any-harm bounds alongside this comparative requirement; even a comparative pass does not certify a low absolute harm rate. A model can fail the harmful-acceptance criterion despite higher mean Dice.

The optional soft stage advances only if it beats the no-soft early schedule under the **final hard policy**, with the same compute accounting and harm/coverage constraints. A soft-training loss decrease alone is not evidence. C advances only after G3 and replay prerequisites; choosing C because it is more expressive is not a preregistered reason.

## 9. Compute, VRAM, latency, and reproducibility protocol

Record hardware model/VRAM, driver, CUDA/cuDNN, framework/compiler versions, source/config/checkpoint hashes, seeds, precision, TF32/determinism flags, batch size, autocast, memory layout, device placement, and whether kernels are compiled. No machine availability or checkpoint compatibility is assumed in this report.

For inference, measure batch-1 slice latency and full-patient volume latency. First time cold load/compile separately, then warm up with 20 complete representative rollouts. Measure at least 100 timed slice rollouts from a fixed, shuffled patient/phase sample; synchronize the GPU around host-timed regions or use correctly synchronized device events. Report median/p90/p95, mean, IQR and the number of runs, with fixed-patient paired order randomized across methods to reduce thermal/order bias. Report both natural hard-policy latency and forced-cap latency; include encoder, decoder, auditor, history writes, crop/branch selection, sparse scatter/gather, CC coordinate gradients, replay and restitution checks. Report preprocessing/IO separately plus full end-to-end wall time.

For memory, reset peak statistics after warmup and use a fresh process per method/config to avoid allocator-history confusion. Record peak allocated and reserved GPU memory, host RSS, persistent caches, maximum event count and live replay graphs. Measure training peak separately for a full forward/backward/optimizer step, fixed physical batch size, same unroll cap, and the exact auxiliary losses. C that needs an inference-time coordinate gradient cannot run wholly in inference mode; profile that graph and no-gradient phases correctly. A small hidden-state tensor is not the model's peak VRAM.

For operation counts, one MAC is one multiply-accumulate; disclose any 2-FLOPs/MAC conversion. Count convolution/matmul MACs analytically and independently compare to a profiler; list unsupported operations such as grid sampling, indexing, scatter, top-k, synchronization and nonlinearity instead of reporting them as zero cost. Report encoder-once, initial decoder, ordinary turn, worst-case turn, mean natural rollout, and full-volume costs. State bytes, replay storage and sparse metadata are separate from weight counts. For training report total labelled/unlabelled presentations, gradient steps, forward/backward calls, total accelerator hours and measured or estimated total operations, with estimates labelled.

Archive a per-patient outcome table and raw count ledger, plus per-transition identifiers, previous/candidate/retained annotations, predicted audit scores/maps or sufficient reproducible references, acceptance/HALT, state intervention id, candidate pool identity and history-validity support. Save exact proposed/final thresholds, calibration grid results and frozen-model provenance. A one-patient visualization can illustrate where a fix was destroyed; it cannot substitute for paired patient statistics. No scientific results, GPU numbers, or readiness claims are supplied by this planning study.
