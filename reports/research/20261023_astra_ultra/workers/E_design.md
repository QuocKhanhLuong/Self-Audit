## Decision

## Only two central questions

**Q1 — Allocation:** once the local solver is numerically qualified, does separate class allocation provide a useful update beyond uniformly scaling the same aggregate pseudo direction, with exactly the same finite classwise guard and audit-label access?

**Q2 — Patient utility:** if Q1 survives, does the complete guarded training policy improve fixed-budget patient segmentation sufficiently to justify its compute, relative to same-block plain SSL, extra supervised optimization, and the stronger full-EMA baseline? A current-audit Brier improvement alone does not answer Q2.

The estimand for Q2 is the paired difference in final-student, dense raw **patient-macro foreground Dice** between methods under a frozen L8 data/preprocessing/training recipe. For patient p, first average each class's reference-present frame Dice within that patient under the explicit absent-reference omission policy, then average RV/MYO/LV, then average patients. Retain classwise differences, absent-reference counts/FPs and RV false negatives; do not substitute pooled-pixel Dice or a thresholded/gated score. This fixed algorithmic recipe is the target, not the best checkpoint or the best seed discovered afterward.

## Minimum matched arms

The minimum allocation contrast has **three** policies. For a **same-state frozen transaction comparison**, they receive identical supervised reference theta_s, q, strong U view, valid/accepted denominator Z and unique class directions d_j, with the same block, pseudo LR, risk budget and tolerance. This equality cannot persist across independently trained policy arms: once their commits differ, their student, teacher q, accepted pixels and candidate directions diverge. In a fresh full-run J−S comparison those later differences are mediated effects of the policy, not matched fixed candidate inputs. Keep frozen-transaction mechanism attribution separate from total training-policy utility.

1. **P — `ema_block` (available):** apply the full aggregate D = sum_j d_j without finite risk guarding. This identifies the value of the complete allocation-plus-guard policy relative to its same-block proposal.
2. **S — aggregate scalar plus identical finite guard (PROPOSED, unavailable):** restrict alpha = a·1, a in [0,1]. Use scalar moments A_c^S = sum_jk A_cjk and b_c^S = sum_j b_cj. To be the exact restriction of the joint regularized objective, use scalar ridge **m·lambda**, where m is the number of nonempty source directions, not lambda. Apply the identical exact functional classwise guard/backtracks to aD. A cheap implementation needs one aggregate gradient/JVP, while a diagnostic implementation can obtain the same moments by summing joint moments; qualify their parity separately.
3. **J — `joint_guard` (available method, numerically unqualified):** solve for separate class coefficients and apply the same finite guard.

S versus J isolates allocation freedom; P versus S combines scalar local choice with finite protection. `independent_guard`, `sign`, and `joint_no_class` do not replace S. Scalar and joint have the same ridge along their shared one-dimensional subspace; otherwise a ridge mismatch could manufacture an advantage.

For a patient-utility claim retain **`supervised_extra` and `ema_full`** as the two additional reference methods. They make a five-arm development screen, not a nine-arm matrix. `supervised` is already the within-transaction reference and a useful retained context, but a fresh sixth full run is not necessary for the primary J−S contrast. The prospective schedule correction below requires fresh matched references: historical completed scores remain contextual. Only an explicitly unchanged-protocol study could consider their reuse after disabled-path/source and provenance compatibility checks. A modified initialization, input, schedule, loss or optimizer requires a fresh matched reference.

A **direct audit-gradient control** is a valuable later discriminator and is currently unavailable: D_A = −eta·grad_B mean_c R_c(theta_s), using the same Brier groups/labels, block, direct update and finite guard. It tests whether extra audit-supervised optimization explains any benefit. It cannot share the pseudo-direction cone and must be called a direction-family comparator. Existing `supervised_extra` changes loss, optimizer, scope and clipping, so it is only a coarse label-use control. No direct-gradient, scalar-guard, fixed role-separated audit, schedule-replay, or exact compute-stop option exists in the present CLI; adding a JSON field will be rejected.

## Cheapest sequence with fixed stopping rules

### Gate 0 — source and synthetic qualification; possible now

Before patient compute, require the solver to recover analytic scalar/rank-degenerate/compensation solutions across the scale range seen in manufactured gradients, with separately stated feasibility and optimality tolerances. Test common rescaling with consistently rescaled ridge/budgets; test an exactly zero solution and a small but useful nonzero one. Validate S as the alpha=a·1 restriction, including m·lambda and empty-class handling. Check functional rejection leaves model/buffers/optimizer/EMA unchanged, one accepted commit is applied directly, and one EMA update follows. Verify P's summed class gradient equals one ordinary pseudo SGD gradient with the same accepted denominator and padding mask.

Also log effective parameter-displacement norm, count of changed parameter elements, and predicted/actual probability displacement: `scale > 0` alone need not imply a representable weight change. Do not tune thresholds to make a pilot accept more updates. Any systematic feasible-but-suboptimal solver termination, rollback mismatch, source duplication, label-boundary violation or schedule mismatch **stops** the efficacy program until corrected and requalified. A repaired solver is an implementation change, not a demonstrated patient improvement. B also supplies manufactured counterexamples where decreasing every audit Brier group still reduces same-audit hard Dice, and where a favorable local solution genuinely has no safe positive finite step. Keep the outcome mismatch and nonlinear infeasibility distinct from the numerical false-stall failure.

Pre-generate separate independently shuffled balanced patient schedules for L and U, plus the audit schedule (uniform from L excluding the current supervised ID), with separate reproducible RNG streams for role schedules and crop/intensity draws. The schedule-generation seed is fixed at 20261023 and distinct from model initialization seed42; preserve that schedule when changing initialization seed. Record the L×U and U×audit incidence tables. This is a **proposed schedule implementation**, unavailable in the current CLI, and a declared new factor relative to the retained phase-locked runs. The old schedule can be demonstrated/qualified using source and synthetic traces without funding another full patient arm. For the cheapest Q1 mechanism study, execute **one P reference trajectory** from fresh initialization. At each of its 144 post-warmup supervised references, evaluate P/S/J on cloned/frozen transaction state with the same q/directions/audit, record all three outcomes, then commit only P and update its EMA once. This is a **proposed functional replay harness**, not supported by current train.py. It tests S/J liveness conditional on the P trajectory; it does not estimate their on-policy EMA evolution or final accuracy. Do not shorten warmup to eight or restart the old native-B16 job. The existing 72-step pilot is capacity evidence only.

Predeclare four separate inactivity categories: no accepted candidate pixels, true local zero optimum confirmed by reference check, numerical failure/suboptimal solve, and finite-trial rejection/rounding. Record moments/eigenvalue scales, alpha, source population, group positive/negative support, local-to-actual risk error, trial count and effective parameter delta. The current log records some of these but **does not save A,b/eigenvalues or effective delta**; extend diagnostics before the pilot.

Stop immediately for nonfinite predictions, out-of-budget **guard-accepted S/J replay** group risk, silent fallback/OOM recipe change, source/hash mismatch, outside-L label reads, or state contamination. P is deliberately unguarded: log its risk regressions but do not abort for that alone. If J yields **no accepted effective nonzero replay displacement across all 144 eligible post-warmup transactions**, it is a liveness NO-GO for this recipe; distinguish candidate absence from the other categories and do not call it general algorithmic impossibility. If there are **zero multi-source transactions**, Q1 is uninformative for allocation freedom: stop expansion because the hypothesized mechanism lacks measured opportunity, without claiming S/J equivalence. If multi-source transactions exist but S and J produce equal committed predictions within qualified numerical tolerance on every one, do not fund a multi-source novelty study on this recipe. Predeclare this as resource triage, not a statistical hypothesis test. A single interesting transaction is insufficient evidence of utility.

The reported ~13.47h/10k instrumented block run is an **observed timing reference, not a ceiling** for guarded methods or replay. Solver/trial overhead can make them slower, and warmup work differs. Profile the actual pilot and set a written GPU-hour cap before any full runs. No numerical cap is claimed approved in this report.

### Gate 2 — one frozen development screen, then narrow replication

Only if Gate 1 survives, run the minimum P/S/J fixed 10k endpoints plus the two additional utility references under the identical prospective shuffled recipe: five fresh arms for the initial block. The new schedule prevents reuse of old endpoints as matched controls; they remain explicitly historical context. If funding cannot cover this, execute only J/S and restrict the claim to allocation under that recipe; do not infer superiority over plain/full SSL or extra supervision. Do not choose the best of 1k/2k/.../10k; the endpoint remains 10k. ## Audit reuse, evaluation and compute accounting

The rotating audit patient is disjoint from the supervised patient only **within that transaction**. Its masks return later to both roles. Native slice/crop observations from the same patient are dependent, and the 50%-foreground sampling plus class-balanced Brier defines a selected TRAIN risk. It does not certify full-volume Dice. Equal audit-read allowance across methods and all masks charged to L are necessary, but do not remove adaptive reuse.

A low-cost *diagnostic* is to evaluate every proposed frozen displacement on a second predetermined L slice/crop and a different L patient without letting those measurements affect coefficients, guard decisions, schedule or checkpoint choice. This distinguishes immediate support sensitivity, not unseen generalization. The current trainer has no shadow-audit option. A stronger role-separated 6-supervised/2-audit partition, rotated by predeclared patient folds within the same eight annotated patients, is also unavailable and changes training exposure; all comparison arms must receive the same partition and label budget. Do not use extra U/VAL masks to create either control. Avoid claiming audit overfitting solely from a low current-audit risk: compare the unseen-in-step diagnostics and final external patients.

Evaluate dense final-student predictions once per frozen recipe, with all predictions hashed before opening references. Existing evaluator already supports `development` and `external_frozen` manifests, patient-macro class Dice, frame metrics and confusion; it has no physical HD95 certification. UNKNOWN predictions, if separately reported, remain foreground misses. Primary evaluation has no threshold. A paired patient bootstrap on the 18 outcomes is conditional on the fitted runs; it does not measure label-subset or training-seed variability. Report block-level paired effects separately and preserve patient/frame nesting. A four-block interval is fragile; show all block effects rather than manufacture a narrow pixel-level interval. The finite-sample model-selection bias motivation is documented by [Cawley and Talbot (JMLR, 2010)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html); retraining variance in medical models is directly demonstrated by [Bosma et al. (MIDL, 2024)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v227/bosma24a.html).

VAL18 is already exposed development data. Freezing the next endpoint prevents an additional checkpoint-selection confound but cannot make VAL18 untouched again. Any confirmation requires a prospectively frozen independent patient/domain set and separate data/evaluation access. TEST filenames existing remotely are not authorization or evidence. No new data are needed for the source questions; genuine generalization beyond this exposed cohort needs additional independent evaluation data.

For every arm report: startup/hash time, measured training-loop and total wall time, GPU model/runtime, peak allocated/reserved memory, teacher forward count, supervised optimizer steps, pseudo gradient count, JVPs, solver time, finite trial forwards, checkpoint/evaluation time, accepted and **effective nonzero** pseudo updates, L/U/audit patient coverage, distinct frame/slice/crop counts, accepted valid pixels by pseudo class and actual labeled frames/slices. Separate diagnostic overhead from an optimized deployment implementation. Do not call the 13.47h instrumented plain baseline optimized. Equal steps are the primary optimization-budget comparison; a compute-matched curve is a separate estimand.

No wall-time stopping exists in the trainer. For a secondary equal-compute comparison, set a common hour budget from an independent profile, translate it to predeclared per-method steps, and report realized deviation; do not choose steps from favorable validation outcomes. A genuinely exact time-budget stop and checkpoint is proposed infrastructure. Every change to schedule/steps is explicit. `supervised_extra` alone does not solve this problem.
