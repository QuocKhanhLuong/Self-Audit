### Main finding

A stronger student may outperform a weak teacher readout by removing nuisance variation, exploiting legitimate image structure that the readout discarded, or using an appropriate inductive bias. None of these is implied by model capacity, target confidence, repeated fitting, or low teacher-student disagreement. The same conditional averaging operation can either improve or worsen true risk: it helps when teacher variation is nuisance and hurts when it contains genuine predictive information absent from the student's inference input. This distinction is measurable in manufactured controls but generally latent without truth in the actual setting.

The most useful next research object is a **fixed-proposal, matched-transfer experiment** with an explicit information contract, followed only conditionally by feedback. Keep proposal quality and student transfer separate; a good proposal may fail transfer, and perfect transfer may faithfully reproduce a bad proposal. Named RV/MYO/LV identity requires a semantic constraint justified independently of feature similarity and consistency.

## Notation and information accounting

Work on a fixed normalized evaluation measure over cines and valid pixels, with the pixel index included in the input. Let `Y ∈ {e_BG, e_RV, e_MYO, e_LV}` be the latent one-hot truth. Let `H` denote all legitimate information available to the teacher/proposal, `V` the student inference information, and `V = v(H)` where a nesting assumption is needed. All predictors are simplex-valued; `η_V = E[Y | V]`, `η_H = E[Y | H]`. A teacher target is `T = T(H)` unless extra randomness is declared. `W ≥ 0` includes target reliability, valid support, activation and the normalization induced by the sampling/loss scheme. Write `||f||_2² = E ||f||_Euclidean²` on the stated measure. Brier risk is `R(f) = E ||f - Y||_Euclidean²`. No claim below equates Brier improvement with Dice improvement.

The actual normalized-per-batch loss induces its own effective weighting. For batch size one, a random within-image denominator must be absorbed into W. An unweighted population theorem is not automatically a theorem about the current reliability-weighted training loss. Frozen TRAIN empirical fit also is not population/new-patient fit.

| Training signal | Information legitimately available to learn | Ambiguity that remains |
|---|---|---|
| Full Q | Full distribution including historical prior/uniform mass, as weighted by current support | Prior mass is not a named observation; errors and unsupported class identity persist |
| Conditional Z with valid h/provenance | Conditional source distribution only on justified support | Conditioning discards unsupported mass; low h amplifies noise and selection effects |
| Allowed-label set/partial constraint | Exclusions, sums, topology, coarse measurements that actually constrain outputs | Multiple feasible named/native maps unless constraints identify a singleton |
| Full-view feature cosine | Teacher feature direction predictable from the student's image/context | No named head is implied; shared parameters can change semantic logits in either direction |
| Feature relations/Gram | Similarity geometry, up to transformations preserving the relation | Orthogonal gauge and label permutations; no independent named evidence |
| Masked view → full target | Predictable conditional components of the full target from visible input | Missing unpredictable bits become a conditional average, not recovered detail |
| Temporal context | Extra observations if actual time frames are input at inference and alignment is valid | Motion, phase, missing frames and noninvertible masking; single-frame inference cannot use unseen context |
| EMA/history | Aggregation/regularization of the same data and parameter trajectory | Common bias and label permutations remain; parameter EMA is not output averaging |

A fixed single-cine inference method may use frames from that cine if declared. A fixed single-frame method may not borrow unseen frames or a target cohort. Training-time privileged context may improve estimation efficiency, but cannot remove irreducible conditional uncertainty at inference. Cross-cine fitted test statistics would be target-domain adaptation and require a different contract.

## Evidence cards

### R4-C1 — Weighted distillation learns a conditional average, not missing sample-specific information

- **Status:** `DERIVED_RESULT` (classical proper-loss conditional minimization; no novelty claim).
- **Plain statement:** Unlimited student capacity cannot reconstruct a target distinction that its input fails to distinguish. Reliability weighting changes which conditional average is learned.
- **Scope/assumptions:** Any fixed joint distribution of `(V,T,W)`, `T ∈ Δ_K`, integrable W, unconstrained measurable prediction `p(V)`, `0 < a(V) = E[W | V] < ∞` on the evaluated set. Minima may lie on the simplex boundary; strictly positive softmax outputs approach rather than attain such minima. No optimization/generalization guarantee is assumed.
- **Formal claim:** For `L(p) = E[-W Σ_c T_c log p_c(V)]`, the conditional optimum is `m_W(V) = E[W T | V] / a(V)`. Moreover `L(p) - L(m_W) = E[a(V) KL(m_W(V) || p(V))]`. On `{a = 0}` the semantic objective imposes no condition.
- **Proof:** Condition on V, pull each `log p_c(V)` outside the conditional expectation, and factor a(V). Cross entropy equals target entropy plus KL. Nonnegativity and equality conditions of KL finish the proof. This is an output-space statement, not a guarantee that shared-encoder AdamW reaches the minimizer.
- **Q/Z corollary:** If, and only if, the declared decomposition `Q = (1-h)u + hZ` is valid with a fixed uniform u, then `m_Q = (1-h̄_W)u + h̄_W z_Wh`, where `h̄_W = E[Wh | V]/E[W | V]` and `z_Wh = E[WhZ | V]/E[Wh | V]` where the denominator is positive. Training on Z with W alone instead learns `E[WZ | V]/E[W | V]`, generally another target. Thus `full Q`, `Z weighted by W`, and `Z weighted by Wh` are distinct policies. Historical banks need not supply this decomposition at all.
- **Counterexample:** V is constant; a hidden fair bit selects T = e_RV or e_LV. Every deterministic student minimizes CE at their half-half mixture and suffers log 2 CE. A larger decoder cannot determine the bit. Separately, unbiased scalar targets .3/.7 with weights 1/3 yield a weighted optimum .6 although unweighted mean/truth is .5. Reliability can introduce conditional bias even when unweighted targets are unbiased.
- **Closest prior:** Generalized distillation [P2] makes the privileged-training/test-input distinction explicit. Noise2Noise [P1, Section 2, Equations (3)–(6)] gives the analogous conditional-mean result for squared reconstruction loss. The present CE calculation is elementary.
- **Code/evidence:** Current weighted CE/transport references above; manufactured `selection_bias`, `erased_cue`, `q_z_decomposition` checks.
- **Implication:** Declare V, teacher H, effective W and intended target before comparing capacity. A masked teacher-target objective should report uncertainty caused by the masked input, rather than interpret residual CE as model weakness.
- **Limitations:** Auxiliary losses and parameter sharing couple outputs, finite samples may be memorized, and fitting m_W can still be scientifically wrong. Uniform dilution changes calibration even where argmax is unchanged for a single point.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** On the finite hidden-bit probe, both a table lookup and any exact population optimizer with constant V return the mixture; exposing the bit eliminates the information obstruction. If a supposed constant-input student recovers the bit, inspect leakage or sampling imbalance.

### R4-C2 — An exact criterion distinguishes teacher denoising from loss of privileged truth signal

- **Status:** `DERIVED_RESULT` with constructive positive/negative cases; standard conditional-projection algebra, not a novel theorem claim.
- **Plain statement:** Student averaging beats a noisy readout when removed variation is sufficiently unrelated to truth. The same averaging loses when the removed variation is useful truth information.
- **Scope/assumptions:** `V ⊆ H`, T is H-measurable, square integrable, `m = E[T | V]`; unweighted risk or one explicitly common reweighted probability measure for all terms. Independent test-distribution statement, not training-residual inference. Let `s² = ||T-m||_2²`, `c = E⟨T-m, η_H-η_V⟩`, `b = ||m-η_V||_2`.
- **Formal claim:** `R(T) - R(m) = s² - 2c`. For any V-measurable student p with `||p-m||_2 ≤ δ`, `R(p) - R(T) ≤ -s² + 2c + 2bδ + δ²`. Thus the sufficient strict-improvement condition is `s² - 2c > 2bδ + δ²`. All of c, b and true-risk improvement are latent in the no-GT task; even δ is a population transfer term unless an appropriate generalization bound is supplied.
- **Proof:** Set r = T-m, so `E[r | V] = 0`. Expand `||T-Y||² = ||m-Y||² + ||r||² + 2⟨r,m-Y⟩`. The term involving m vanishes after conditioning on V. Since r is H-measurable, `E⟨r,Y⟩ = E⟨r,η_H⟩ = E⟨r,η_H-η_V⟩`. For p, write d = p-m; `R(p)-R(m) = ||d||_2² + 2E⟨d,m-η_V⟩`, bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz. Combine.
- **Positive noise-tolerant case:** If T = η_V + b(V) + ε, `E[ε | V] = 0`, and `E⟨ε,Y-η_V⟩ = 0`, then c = 0. Nonzero systematic bias is allowed; student can beat teacher whenever `s² > 2||b||_2 δ + δ²`. Student cannot remove b merely by fitting the same target. A concrete binary example with truth probability .7 and teacher values .5/.9 has teacher Brier .50, exact averaged student .42; the .08 gain is nuisance variance removal.
- **Constructive contrary case:** Keep identical V and identical teacher target values .5/.9, but now the hidden state sets true conditional class probabilities to .5/.9. T is the Bayes predictor given H; averaging yields student Brier .42 versus teacher .34, a .08 loss. Here c = s². Observed `(V,T)` alone cannot distinguish these two truth worlds. A stronger student and repeated EMA cannot resolve that ambiguity.
- **Closest prior:** Noise2Noise [P1] is the direct conditional-noise collision. Generalized distillation [P2] distinguishes privileged training information; Phuong–Lampert [P3, Theorems 1–2] derives teacher recovery/projection for linear teachers/students rather than arbitrary truth improvement. The exact covariance identity here makes the comparison explicit but is classical Hilbert-space algebra.
- **Code/evidence:** `nuisance_denoising` and `privileged_information_loss` exact finite-distribution probe checks.
- **Implication:** A claim that a larger image-only student beats a full-cine teacher needs a reason teacher residuals are nuisance, or an additional image-derived constraint exposing the teacher readout's mistake. Teacher-student disagreement cannot by itself establish that reason.
- **Limitations:** No Dice bound, no all-class guarantee, no finite-sample rate, no mechanism proving the current Q residual is nuisance. For reliability-weighted fitting but unweighted evaluation, replace m by m_W and carry the resulting bias; do not transplant the displayed criterion unchanged.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Two manufactured worlds with identical observed `(V,T)` reverse the teacher/student Brier ordering. Any no-GT certificate using only those observables must give the same verdict to both and therefore cannot certify strict truth improvement in general.

### R4-C3 — Partial evidence and feature agreement constrain equivalence classes, not named anatomy

- **Status:** `DERIVED_RESULT` / `COUNTEREXAMPLE`.
- **Plain statement:** Partial constraints can be useful without assigning every pixel a false label, but they need explicit identifiability assumptions. Features may contain recoverable structure while a relation-matching objective leaves its semantic interpretation undetermined.
- **Scope/assumptions:** A known linear observation B acts on a native probability field p. Partial label sets S and relation matrices are observed legitimately. The identities of unknown anatomical classes are not supplied implicitly by a pretrained task head.
- **Formal claims/proofs:** (i) `Bp=q` identifies at most the feasible set `F_q = {p ∈ Δ_K^n : Bp=q}`. If a nonzero d belongs to `ker B` and both p and p+d remain feasible, both explain the observations exactly. Two native binary cells (1,0) and (0,1) have identical coarse mean .5. (ii) A single partial-set loss `-log Σ_{c∈S}p_c` is minimized by every distribution supported on S; when `|S|>1` it does not name the class. Multiple constraints can identify only if their intersection/objective genuinely removes the ambiguity. (iii) For feature rows F and an orthogonal matrix O, `(FO)(FO)^T = FF^T`; relation matching cannot identify absolute feature axes. A permutation of named semantic output channels also leaves unlabeled geometry/consistency objectives unchanged.
- **Cosine qualification:** Current direct cosine is not Gram matching and does constrain feature direction. For normalized nonzero target t̂ and unit predicted direction s(V), expected weighted cosine is maximized at `s ∥ E[W t̂ | V]` when that mean is nonzero; when it is zero all directions tie. Even exact direction matching supplies a representation, not a named cardiac readout. A semantic head must receive legitimate named information or an explicitly justified image/anatomical prior.
- **Counterexample:** Two bright chambers have the same observed shapes/features and exchange RV/LV names under two possible ontology mappings. All feature, reconstruction, relation and equivariance losses coincide. A class-specific anatomical premise may break the symmetry, but then that premise is supervision/inductive bias to declare and falsify, not evidence manufactured by confidence.
- **Closest prior:** RKD [P6, Section 3.2, Equations (7)–(11)] transfers distances/angles with task losses. DINOv3 [P9, Section 4.2, Equation (2)] already uses Gram anchoring and explicitly allows features to move while preserving similarities. Wei et al. [P5, Theorem 3.6] explicitly retains label-permutation ambiguity in the unsupervised case. No claim of novelty for these invariances.
- **Code/evidence:** Current feature cosine `runner.py:194–222`; manufactured `coarse_nullspace` check. No production relation loss was added.
- **Implication:** A proposed image-energy or temporal constraint should be specified as a shrinkage of F_q and accompanied by an example where it uniquely resolves truth and an example where it selects the wrong feasible field. Preserve missing support as unknown, not BG.
- **Limitations:** Nonlinear priors may identify some cases; this card does not claim all unsupervised segmentation is impossible. It rejects identifying named truth from symmetric observations alone.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A class-permutation or native-swap manufactured control leaves the listed unlabeled objectives unchanged; a claim of named improvement based only on them must fail this control.

### R4-C4 — Noisy, biased proposal feedback can contract error, with an explicit floor and transfer budget

- **Status:** `DERIVED_RESULT`; any corresponding method is `PROPOSED_NOT_APPROVED`.
- **Plain statement:** Feedback can improve a student even with nonzero proposal bias and noise, but only if the proposal's truth-related contraction is strong enough and transfer error is controlled. Merely obtaining a fixed point or decreasing a proxy is insufficient.
- **Scope/assumptions:** Fix a common measure and latent η(V). At round t let p_t be the current predictor. A detached proposal A_t is produced using a declared observation/constraint/inductive bias. Conditional on the current history ℱ_t, let `μ_t = E[A_t | ℱ_t]` satisfy `||μ_t-η||_2 ≤ ρ ||p_t-η||_2 + β`, with `0 ≤ ρ < 1`, `β ≥ 0`; let `E[||A_t-μ_t||_2² | ℱ_t] ≤ σ²`. Use output-space relaxation `B_t = (1-α)p_t + αA_t`, `0 < α ≤ 1`. Actual trained student obeys `E[||p_{t+1}-B_t||_2² | ℱ_t] ≤ δ_t²`. All norm statements use the same population measure. These are assumptions to justify, not metrics inferred from consistency loss.
- **Formal claim:** With `e_t = ||p_t-η||_2` and `a = 1-α+αρ < 1`,

  `sqrt(E[||p_{t+1}-η||_2² | ℱ_t]) ≤ sqrt((a e_t + αβ)² + α²σ²) + δ_t ≤ a e_t + αβ + ασ + δ_t`.

  Consequently, if unconditional RMS errors `E_t = sqrt(E ||p_t-η||_2²)` and a uniform δ bound are used, `E_{t+1} ≤ a E_t + α(β+σ) + δ`, hence `E_t ≤ a^t E_0 + [α(β+σ)+δ](1-a^t)/(1-a)`. The latter is conservative; independent zero-mean noise admits the sharper first inequality. Strict one-step improvement is sufficient when `sqrt((a e_t + αβ)² + α²σ²) + δ_t < e_t`.
- **Proof:** Write `A_t = μ_t + ξ_t`; conditional mean zero makes the cross term between ξ_t and `(1-α)(p_t-η)+α(μ_t-η)` vanish in conditional squared L2 risk. Triangle inequality bounds the latter mean term by `a e_t+αβ`. Apply Minkowski to add transfer error. Iterating the affine upper bound yields the geometric series. This proof concerns predictions, not projected gradients, parameter EMA, AdamW displacement, or nonconvex optimization.
- **Constructive biased/noisy example:** Scalar probability η=.7, p=.9, ρ=.5, β=.02, symmetric noise ±.03, α=.4 gives expected squared error .028368 versus .04 before mixing. This has nonzero bias and noise. These truth parameters are manufactured for the probe; they are latent for real cines.
- **Failure/counterexample:** A_t=p_t gives ρ=1, β=σ=0, no contraction. A_t is an overconfident repeated wrong prediction can increase true risk despite lower hard-target CE. A globally swapped-label fixed point can have perfect consistency and poor named accuracy. If proposal bias β dominates current error, feedback can degrade an already good student.
- **Closest prior:** No novelty claim for contraction-with-perturbation recurrences. Wei et al. [P5, Assumptions 3.3/4.1, Theorem 4.3] supplies a more substantive self-training result under class expansion, separation and bounded per-class pseudo-label error. Mobahi et al. [P4, Sections 3.3–3.6] supplies another positive mechanism: changing effective regularization without receiving new task information. Our recurrence is bookkeeping until a concrete proposal mechanism establishes ρ, β and σ.
- **Code/evidence:** `noisy_biased_feedback`, `hardening_error_amplification`; current production has no such update path.
- **Implication:** Keep feedback disabled until an explicit proposal mechanism has survived manufactured common-bias and native-swap controls and the one-shot transfer experiment. Even then, no-GT controls can falsify obvious defects, not certify latent contraction.
- **Limitations:** The uniform norm bound may fail on RV while holding globally. For per-class conclusions use class-restricted measures and constants; such measures/constants themselves depend on truth. Correlated history is allowed only through the stated conditional moment assumptions, never through an independence fiction.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Increasing proposal corruption β, σ or transfer δ in manufactured worlds crosses an explicit improvement boundary. A feedback claim insensitive to these perturbations is measuring proxy stability, not the displayed truth-risk mechanism.

### R4-C5 — EMA, history and masks can regularize; repeating evidence does not create an independent observation

- **Status:** `DERIVED_RESULT`, `COUNTEREXAMPLE`, with bounded `LITERATURE_RESULT` support.
- **Plain statement:** Self-distillation need not improve information to improve generalization, but a claim of improvement must name the regularization/observation mechanism. Repeated copies preserve bias; destructive masks can erase the very cue needed for a named label.
- **Scope/assumptions:** Exact population self-target fitting, or output averaging with explicitly stated joint noise moments. Parameter averaging is a distinct nonlinear operation.
- **Formal claims/proofs:** If T=p_t(V) and there is no changed input, weighting, function restriction or auxiliary objective, C1 gives p_{t+1}=p_t. For outputs `A_j=η+b+ε_j`, normalized nonnegative weights a_j give mean error `b+Σ_j a_j ε_j`; if zero-mean noises are uncorrelated with common second moment σ², variance is `σ²Σ_j a_j²`. For arbitrary dependence it is `Σ_{ij} a_i a_j E⟨ε_i,ε_j⟩`; identical errors give no variance reduction. A stationary ideal output EMA with decay γ and independent innovations has variance multiplier `(1-γ)/(1+γ)`, while unchanged b survives. This formula is not a theorem about neural-network weight EMA. Masked-view CE returns C1's conditional target average; if masks erase a sole fair predictive bit, minimum Brier becomes .5 and CE log 2, although the unmasked Bayes predictor has zero risk.
- **Counterexample:** One hundred identical predictions .8 with true binary probability .2 still average to .8. Hardening an incorrect .6 probability to 1 increases full Brier from .72 to 2 for a negative-class truth, while hard-target CE can become zero. These are calibration/error-propagation failures, not claims that argmax error changes in that exact example.
- **Closest prior:** Mean Teacher [P7, Section 2 and Appendix B.1] uses weight EMA, supervised classification and consistency; it is not annotation-free semantic identification. Mobahi et al. [P4, Theorems 5–6 and Section 3.6] shows self-distillation changes regularization in its Hilbert-space setting and notes exact interpolation leaves rounds unchanged. MAE [P8, Section 3] predicts masked raw pixels and subsequently evaluates transfer; it does not establish cardiac named masks from reconstruction. DINOv3 [P9, Sections 3.2/4.2/5.2] has carefully constructed representation objectives, not just repeated named pseudo-target fitting.
- **Code/evidence:** `history_common_bias`, `erased_cue`, `hardening_error_amplification` checks; no EMA implementation enabled in inspected training path.
- **Implication:** Keep duplicates grouped by source lineage; their agreement is not independent corroboration. A static image mask adds an inductive bias/problem restriction, not a new sensor observation. Temporal frames add observations only when truly available and their relationship to the labeled structure is justified.
- **Limitations:** Even dependent averaging can help; the covariance formula determines it. Repeated fitting with explicit regularization can improve generalization without any new data, so the correct rejection is "repetition alone supplies no information," not "self-distillation can never improve."
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Duplicating a fixed biased source must not increase an evidence-support score; replacing independent innovations by identical copies removes the predicted variance gain.

### R4-C6 — Proposal quality and student transfer are separate experimental quantities

- **Status:** `DERIVED_RESULT`; staged design `PROPOSED_NOT_APPROVED`.
- **Plain statement:** Demonstrate a proposal improves the desired output before attributing a student's result to successful self-learning; then measure how much of that improvement transfers under exactly the same inference information and compute constraints.
- **Scope/assumptions:** Let A be a frozen V-measurable proposal, p0 a frozen baseline, and p a transferred student. All norms/risk use the same valid grid/measure. Let `d = ||p-A||_2` and `a0 = ||A-η_V||_2`.
- **Formal claim/proof:** `R(p) ≤ R(A) + 2a0d + d²` by expanding p=A+(p-A) and conditioning Y on V. Thus a proposal advantage `g = R(p0)-R(A)>0` is preserved if `2a0d+d²<g`. A is a truth-quality object and d a transfer object; small d alone does not imply g>0. If the proposal sees H unavailable to V, the transfer target should first be projected/characterized as in C1–C2; treating pointwise teacher-fit residual as avoidable model error is incorrect.
- **Counterexample:** A perfectly wrong, permuted proposal with d=0 transfers perfectly and fails anatomy. A true proposal with a hidden label bit cannot be transferred pointwise by a constant-input student. Conversely, restricted student denoising can outperform a noisy proposal via C2, so lack of pointwise agreement need not be failure; report both quantities instead of optimizing agreement as the sole quality measure.
- **Closest prior:** Generalized distillation [P2] and RKD [P6] already separate transferable knowledge from model implementation, but their standard task-supervised empirical setting does not certify our annotation-free proposals. The bound is elementary perturbation algebra, not a novelty claim.
- **Code/evidence:** This is an experiment design, not executed training. Current coarse-only baseline remains unchanged.
- **Implication:** Establish fixed proposals, retain source provenance and stop-gradient, freeze controls/settings before any permitted reference scoring, and do not use TEST/VAL to construct or select proposals. A no-GT proposal diagnostic may eliminate bad mechanisms but cannot supply g or a0.
- **Limitations:** Population risks and native truth are unavailable during Phase I. The separation is useful experimental discipline even when no quality claim can yet be made.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A label-permutation control can keep transfer d near zero while inverting proposal truth quality; a hidden-input control creates an irreducible d floor.
