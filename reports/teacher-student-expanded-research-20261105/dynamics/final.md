## A. Same-image graph operator as the decoder

**Mathematical object.** Teacher and student encode exactly the same original image x. Teacher T(x) yields sparse conductances w_ij; student S(x) yields node forcing b_i. In the simplest scalar-channel version,

`L_T = incidence^T diag(w_T) incidence`, `H_T = (I + tau L_T)^(-1) b_S(x)`.

The representation is the solved field H_T, jointly determined by both encoders. Optional vector channels use connection maps R_ij in edge energy `sum w_ij ||H_i - R_ij H_j||²`; raw frame coordinates are not transfer targets. First test the scalar version because arbitrary latent frames introduce an avoidable gauge problem. A native coordinate-query decoder D(H,r,local-intensity) produces all segmentation logits; this is the whole decoder, not correction of an existing mask.

**Transfer and full information graph.** First train a strong teacher, including its graph head, with budgeted TRAIN labels. Freeze it. On labeled TRAIN, execute both `x→T→L_T`, `x→S→(b,L_S)`, then `D(solve(L_T,b))` and `D(solve(L_S,b))`; optimize their segmentation losses with shared S and D. For the ideal exact solve, teacher-conditioned decoding changes the learning signal to student forcing: `d loss/db = (I+tau L_T)^(-T) d loss/dH`. The student graph branch receives its own dense supervision. No feature/gradient agreement term defines the method. This is transfer through shared computation, although improvement over ordinary joint training is **unproven**. If an unlabeled objective is later added, declare it as a separate factor rather than quietly changing this minimal protocol. At inference the only graph is `x,metadata→S→b,L_S→bounded solve→D→mask`; teacher, temporal data and subject fitting are absent.

**Actually different compact architecture.** Use a fixed-resolution depthwise patch encoder, sparse patch-neighbor edges plus a small bank of global latent nodes, four predeclared graph iterations, and a small native-coordinate MLP. There is no down/up pyramid or U-Net skip concatenation. Global nodes communicate over long distances; local edges preserve boundary detail. The deployed object is explicitly H_4: H_0=b, H_(k+1)=H_k+omega[b-(I+tau L)H_k], with normalized scalar graph spectrum in [0,2], 0≤tau≤1 and omega=1/(1+tau). Its error contracts by at most q=tau/(1+tau)≤1/2 per iteration, hence ||H_4-H*||≤q^4||H_0-H*|| in exact arithmetic; no semantic error bound follows. Its gradient uses the actual four-step polynomial, not the inverse formula above. These spectral conditions do not automatically hold for an arbitrary connection implementation. Orthogonal coordinate re-expression should preserve scalar predictions; physical distances use affine metadata and quadrature weights. Arbitrary anatomical warps and voxel resampling are not exact symmetries.

For P patches, E edges, K global nodes, width d and N raster queries, report encoder cost, approximately `O(4(E+PK)d)` message work, state/edge memory, and `O(N·query-MLP-cost)` rasterization. Parameters do not establish speed. Coordinate-query evaluation can dominate at native resolution. The four-step global communication and all interpolation overhead belong in deployment measurements.

**Predicted distinction and failure.** If teacher edges transfer anatomical computation, anatomically randomized edges should remove the benefit even when regularization strength is preserved; pixel-index coordinates should specifically lose robustness to spacing shifts. RKD already transfers distance/angle relations without matching coordinates, so “relational” or “gauge invariant” is not novelty [1]. Here relations actually determine the forward decoder, a narrower hypothesis. OctreeNCA already supplies compact recurrent segmentation with coarse-to-fine communication and specialized kernels [2]; our fixed graph solve and teacher-conditioned forcing must earn their difference experimentally.

A two-node counterexample exposes the danger. For a wrong unit edge and forcing `(1,-3)`, the first scalar output is `-1+2/(1+2 tau)`, becoming negative when `tau>1/2`: a numerically stable graph can erase myocardium. Rare pathological shapes and touching structures make this plausible. For exact PSD solves with common b, `||H_S-H_T|| ≤ tau ||L_S-L_T|| ||H_T||`; this is an operator-sensitivity bound, not a Dice guarantee, and finite solves add approximation error. A persistent teacher/deployment gap kills the transfer claim. Root supplied a FIXTURE_ONLY counterexample in PROBE_RESULTS.json: projecting a resolvent versus solving a projected operator differed by Frobenius norm 0.579636. Therefore patch coarsening must validate operator responses and native boundary support; exact copied adjacency does not imply exact transferred computation. This is reported root fixture evidence, not my execution or patient evidence.

**Cheapest decisive test.** Before patient training, test passive-coordinate equivariance, a one-pixel wall, the two-node counterexample, and finite-solver error on controlled phantoms. Then, if authorized, prelock a small patient pilot comparing equal-compute labeled-only graph student, teacher-conditioned dual path, ordinary KD, and graph smoothing without learned teacher anatomy. Add node-permuted teacher graphs (exact graph-spectrum/degree-multiset control; spatial displacement changes), local degree-preserving rewiring, and blocked gradients from teacher branch into S. Hold teacher quality, labels, initialization and image schedules fixed. Require deployed-path improvement and thin-MYO/pathology safety, not just a strong joint branch.

## B. Temporal privilege: an observable generator distribution

The object is a posterior over anatomical transport generators, `q(A,z|x_t)`, rather than one matrix or a deterministic phase. A separately declared cine teacher sees C and estimates trajectories `z_(t+dt)≈exp(dt A_C)z_t`; same-frame T(x_t) and S(x_t) jointly seed the anatomical decoder. Train observable transport likelihoods, visibility and segmentation, not matrix-entry similarity: A and MAM^-1 describe the same latent dynamics after a basis change. Teacher-conditioned trajectory decoding can train the shared anatomical state; direct single-frame supervised decoding remains mandatory.

Student inference is `x_t→small spatial encoder→mixture of low-rank generator/state parameters→fixed quadrature of anatomical decoder→mask`. A small state bank and coordinate MLP replace a U-Net decoder; each mixture component and raster query is charged to runtime. No temporal teacher, frame index, cine retrieval or subject-specific optimization is permitted in the primary branch. A temporal teacher that produces superior pseudo-labels alone is a stronger-target baseline, not evidence for generator transfer.

**Identifiability limit.** Identical instantaneous images can arise during contraction and relaxation, with velocities +v and -v, or from different through-plane motion. Any deterministic map of that image must assign the same dynamics to both. A mixture may represent uncertainty but cannot identify the patient's true branch. Averaging velocities into zero is not faithful uncertainty. Privileged training cannot reduce the Bayes segmentation risk conditional on x below the information actually in x; it can only improve learning with finite data or impose useful priors. Moreover, if segmentation is independent of generator given morphology, the entire dynamical head may be irrelevant.

Prediction: any single-image gain should concentrate on ambiguous anatomy and survive an identifiability stress test with calibrated multimodal dynamics. Kill this route if a strong cine teacher improves segmentation but the generator-conditioned student does not beat ordinary pseudo-label transfer at equal labels/compute. Cheapest tests pair the same rendered frame with opposite valid trajectories, then compare correct temporal ordering against within-patient order permutations and repeated-frame sequences. These synthetic tests cannot establish clinical benefit.

NISF++ actually models coordinate/time-conditioned anatomy, but uses multiview cine and a patient latent optimized for 2500 inference steps; its image fidelity can improve while segmentation plateaus [3]. That evidence **downgrades** the initial suggestion that fitting dynamics should naturally improve masks. It does not test our amortized uncertain-generator hypothesis.

## C. Reopened alternative: material fields with explicit visibility

Represent tissue by `a(u)` and deformation/observation maps phi_t; visible-frame segmentation is `y_t(r)=a(phi_t^-1(r))`, with visibility v_t and an explicit image-formation likelihood. This is correspondence-driven decoding, not agreement between independently predicted masks. Teacher and student original-frame encoders jointly initialize the material field during TRAIN; the equal-cine student alone estimates field and transport at inference using a small temporal state-space bank and coordinate renderer. It is student-only deployment but **not single-image deployment**. Multi-view acquisitions are a further declared input advantage.

CineMesh4D already combines cine encoders, a fixed-connectivity mesh VAE, temporal fusion and differentiable contour rendering [4]. NISF++ already learns continuous spatiotemporal segmentation fields [3]. Thus neither meshes nor continuous 4D coordinates constitute novelty. Reopen C specifically as an **amortized visibility-aware material-field alternative without a fixed anatomical mesh**, and as an experimental upper-information comparator for B. It might handle disappearing tissue more honestly, but an unconstrained visibility variable can hide every reconstruction error. Prelock a visibility budget and evaluate withheld frames; never call cycle consistency a correspondence certificate.

Its distinguishing prediction is accurate correspondence under transient low contrast and controlled out-of-plane occlusion, beyond equal-cine temporal attention and duplicated-frame controls. Killer cases are symmetric regions that exchange identities while remaining cycle-consistent, topology-changing pathology, and thin walls lost by field smoothing. Require image-consistent visibility and native boundary metrics; prettier motion is insufficient. This route addresses compact redesign and combined teacher/student representation, but fails the primary single-image requirement and offers no established transfer advantage.

## Peer challenge and response

Compilation correctly challenged the mismatch between an ideal inverse and a few deployed iterations. **Accepted:** deployment is the explicit H_4 polynomial above; bounded normalization and contraction replace the vague solver claim. Its proposed thin-ring test will compare exact solve, H_4, equal-matvec numerical alternatives, and an image-edge graph on identical forcing. If teacher advantage disappears under exact solving, attribute it to numerical behavior; if image-edge smoothing ties, reject anatomical transfer. Neither a small residual nor PSD implies correct myocardium. Degree/spectrum controls are necessary but insufficient because alignment with forcing matters.

**Retained with uncertainty:** a small graph-posterior mixture is worth a later ambiguity test because solving the mean graph need not average completed solutions; it may prevent nonexistent bridges created by mean adjacency. This is an optional separate factor with matched total matvec cost, not a replacement central proposal or a single-frame identifiability cure. My reciprocal challenge to compilation is recorded in peer_review.md: program labels need not identify computation, so freeze noncommuting primitive semantics before claiming learned order.

## Four-goal accounting and next decision

A satisfies the proposed same-image fusion and distinct compact architecture goals; B adds explicitly privileged temporal learning; C intentionally changes deployment inputs.
