## 1. Adaptive finite-element segmentation as a learned construction program

**Computational graph.** A small, nonhierarchical image stem produces local samples h_S(x,p); pooled moments summarize the same image. A set of vertices V and a triangulation M partition the physical image domain. Starting from a small cover, a bounded sequential program selects a cell, splits or moves its vertices, and assigns categorical vertex coefficients. The final decoder is barycentric interpolation followed by softmax, not a feature pyramid or a residual correction:

    f_c(p) = sum_{v in vertices(cell(p))} b_v(p) a_{v,c};  q(p)=softmax f(p).

Cells may refine down to native pixel scale. Every prediction is generated by the same mesh representation; there is no coarse prediction plus unrestricted residual. Optional face-specific coefficients permit sharp interfaces, but then continuity and memory must be re-accounted. The primary version uses continuous logits and a fixed maximum of K cells.

**Joint same-image representation and transfer.** At training, each cell carries r_TS(C)=[integral_C h_S, integral_C P h_T, physical edge lengths, current categorical coefficients]. A frozen teacher and budgeted TRAIN truth define a finite-horizon construction oracle: choose the next split that most reduces segmentation error per added cell, respecting the hard K budget. On image-only U, only teacher distributions define the surrogate oracle. The transferred mathematical object is an ordered, conditional geometric program, not equality between feature vectors. A student policy observes r_S(C)=[integral_C h_S, geometry, coefficients] and learns to execute this program, including stop decisions. Crucially, the student path is actually rolled out during training: later actions are relabeled on its visited states so exposure error cannot be hidden by teacher-forced prefixes. The joint representation is used by the privileged planner; the student decoder consumes geometry and coefficients built on its own path.

One formal target is pi*(s)=argmin_a [R(q_{apply(s,a)},t)+lambda deltaK(a)] within a finite candidate set. Full rollout value is preferable to greedy immediate gain because a split may only enable later useful splits. Ground-truth and teacher-only oracle quality must be reported separately. This construction program is not guaranteed recoverable from student features.

**Prediction.** If cardiac boundaries are representable with a small adaptive piecewise-linear partition, learned allocation should spend cells on thin myocardium, apices, and RV junctions while retaining large homogeneous cells. At equal K it should beat uniform triangulation particularly where boundary complexity is concentrated; at matched native quality, fewer cells might offset policy and rasterization overhead. Neither efficiency nor better Dice is assumed.

**Physical/native detail and cost.** Vertices live in millimeters obtained from valid image geometry, and losses use physical area. Rasterize directly at original pixel centers. A slice protocol cannot infer interslice anatomy merely from z spacing. Dense stem and rasterization each cost at least O(N); sequential policy can cost O(K^2 d) naively, or O(K log K) selection plus local updates if priorities are valid. Training an oracle can be much more expensive than inference. K must scale with hard cases; fixed K cannot promise thin-detail preservation.

**Killer counterexample.** A one-pixel-thick winding myocardium or many disconnected components needs nearly native-scale cells everywhere. A compact mesh either erases it or loses its claimed resource advantage. Worse, a confident teacher misses a small RV component; its oracle never allocates there, and perfect program imitation preserves the mistake. Test representational capacity with an oracle mesh before training a policy.

## 2. A recurrent categorical cellular solver with image-conditioned interaction rules

**Computational graph.** Each native pixel has a small hidden state and categorical belief. A shallow stem computes unary evidence u_S and local edge conductances. The same learned local transition is applied for T steps on a native physical adjacency graph; no downsampling encoder-decoder exists. A tiny global broadcast of class-mass and spatial moments may communicate long-range evidence, but is explicit and ablated. The output is the evolved categorical field itself:

    q_i^{t+1}=softmax[-u_i - sum_{j in N(i)} W_ij q_j^t + B m(q^t)],
    m(q)=sum_i area_i psi(p_i)q_i / sum_i area_i.

W_ij is a C-by-C compatibility tensor, not necessarily scalar smoothing. This permits local attraction, exclusion, and class-dependent boundary rules. Shared updates constrain parameter count but T still multiplies work. A damping coefficient or energy-compatible restricted W is necessary if the unrestricted update cycles.

**Joint same-image representation and transfer.** TRAIN-time edge rules depend jointly on h_T(x,i), h_T(x,j), h_S(x,i), h_S(x,j), physical displacement, and image contrast. A teacher-conditioned local energy E_TS(q|x) defines how arbitrary categorical states should evolve. Transfer the induced intervention-response operator, not only the final teacher segmentation: initialize several corrupted categorical fields on the same image, run the teacher-conditioned solver, and fit student transition parameters to repair the same errors over trajectories. The student-only operator E_S is always the deployed graph. A coordinate basis change in h_T that leaves the induced energy invariant should leave transferred behavior unchanged. Teacher feature matching does not have that invariance by construction.

    E(q)=sum_i u_i' q_i + 1/2 sum_(i,j) q_i' W_ij q_j + global(q).

The teacher is not itself necessarily an energy model; producing E_TS is a new learned module trained only on budgeted TRAIN labels, then frozen for U. Its correctness is an assumption to test, not inherited from teacher logits. Dense TRAIN labels used for trajectory scoring count fully.

**Prediction.** Rule transfer should improve recovery from different initial segmentation mistakes compared with output KD, even when final teacher masks are identical. Removing intervention diversity should erase that advantage if operator learning is the mechanism. A student with low local feature capacity might recover long connected thin boundaries via repeated communication; this is a testable hope, not a guarantee.

**Physical/native detail and cost.** Conductances use physical edge lengths and finite-volume weights; do not assume square pixels or silently connect distant slices. Complexity is O(T |E| C^2 + T N d) with explicit global moments O(TNC). Inference memory can be O(N(d+C)); naive backprop retains O(TN(d+C)). Local propagation range is T hops: long-range semantics require larger T or broadcast. Stability must be tested on the deployed precision.

**Killer counterexample.** Two visually identical disconnected cavities require different anatomical names, but their T-hop neighborhoods and global moments agree. The local rule cannot distinguish them. Symmetry breaking through coordinates can fail under orientation or pathology shift; recurrent attraction can delete thin walls, leak across weak boundaries, or cycle. Cheap tests must separate expressive inability from poor optimization.
